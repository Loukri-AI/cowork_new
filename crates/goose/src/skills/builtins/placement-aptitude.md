# Campus placement aptitude

Most Indian campus drives open with a timed aptitude round. Speed matters as
much as accuracy: roughly one minute per question, often with negative
marking.

## Always give the shortcut, not only the long method

The long method proves the answer. The shortcut is what passes the round.
Give both, in that order, and say which to use under time pressure.

## Quantitative topics that carry the most marks

- **Percentages, profit and loss, discount.** Convert percentages to
  fractions: 12.5% is one eighth, 16.67% is one sixth, 6.25% is one
  sixteenth. This turns most sums into one-line mental arithmetic.
- **Ratio, proportion, ages, partnership.** Work in parts, not values.
- **Time and work.** Use total-work-as-LCM. If A takes 12 days and B takes
  18, let the work be 36 units; A does 3 a day, B does 2.
- **Time, speed and distance.** Relative speed for trains and boats. Convert
  km/h to m/s by multiplying by 5/18.
- **Simple and compound interest.** For two years, compound interest exceeds
  simple interest by P times r squared over 10000.
- **Averages and mixtures.** Alligation for any two-group mixing question.
- **Permutations, combinations, probability.** Decide "does order matter"
  before anything else.
- **Numbers.** Divisibility rules, remainders, unit digit cycles, HCF and
  LCM, counting trailing zeros in a factorial.

## Logical reasoning

Seating arrangement, blood relations, syllogisms, coding-decoding, series,
directions, and data sufficiency. For arrangements, draw the diagram and
place only the definite clues first, then branch. For syllogisms, use Venn
diagrams and test whether a conclusion must follow, not whether it could.

## Data interpretation

Read the units and the footnote before the question. Most wrong answers come
from a units mismatch or from using the wrong row, not from arithmetic.
Approximate aggressively when the options are far apart.

## Verbal

Reading comprehension, para jumbles, sentence correction, synonyms and
antonyms. For para jumbles, find the opening sentence (no pronouns, no
connectives) and the mandatory pairs first.

## How to help

Solve the question, then show the shortcut, then state the time it should
take. When a student is practising, give one question at a time and ask for
their answer before revealing yours. If they get it wrong, find the step
where the reasoning broke rather than restating the whole solution.
