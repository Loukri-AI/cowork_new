# Lab records and project reports

## Standard lab record structure

Most JNTU-affiliated and similar colleges expect these headings in order:

1. **Aim.** One sentence, stating what is being determined or verified.
2. **Apparatus.** Each item with its range and least count, since the least
   count decides how many significant figures the result may carry.
3. **Theory.** The governing principle and the formula to be used, with
   every symbol defined and its unit given.
4. **Circuit or setup diagram.** Labelled.
5. **Procedure.** Numbered steps, written in the passive past tense.
6. **Observations.** A table with column headings carrying units.
7. **Calculations.** One worked specimen calculation in full, then the
   table of results for the remaining readings.
8. **Result.** The value obtained with its unit and uncertainty, and whether
   it agrees with theory.
9. **Precautions and sources of error.**

## Error analysis

Give percentage error as the difference from the standard value over the
standard value, times one hundred. Distinguish the two kinds:

- **Systematic**: zero error, instrument calibration, parallax, heat loss
  to surroundings. These shift every reading the same way and can be
  corrected.
- **Random**: reading scatter. These are reduced by repeating and averaging.

Do not report more significant figures than the least count of the coarsest
instrument justifies. This is the most common mark deduction in lab records.

## Project report structure

Title page, certificate, declaration, acknowledgement, abstract, contents,
list of figures and tables, then:

1. Introduction, with the problem statement and objectives
2. Literature survey, with numbered citations
3. System analysis or existing system
4. Proposed methodology, with block diagram
5. Design and implementation
6. Results and discussion
7. Conclusion and future scope
8. References, in a consistent style
9. Appendices for code, datasheets and extended results

## Writing style

Passive voice and past tense for procedure and results. Number every figure
and table, give each a caption, and refer to each at least once in the text.
An uncited figure is treated as padding.

Keep the abstract under 250 words and write it last: it states the problem,
the approach, the main result with a number, and the conclusion.

## How to help

Ask for the actual readings before writing the calculations, and never
invent observation data: fabricated readings are academic misconduct and
graders spot inconsistent values. If a student has no readings yet, write
the structure with the table headings ready and mark where their values go.
